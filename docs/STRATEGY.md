# TGN – The Good Neighbour: strategy and operating plan

*Here when it matters.*

**Status:** draft for discussion with partners, an insurer and a lawyer. It is not legal or financial advice. Every rand figure that
comes from the product is a **placeholder** until real quotes exist. Facts are marked **verified** (read at the source) or **to confirm**.

---

## 1. In one paragraph

TGN is funeral cover that a family builds itself and receives **in kind**: a grocery hamper, and a sheep or a cow, delivered to the
family's door when someone covered passes away. Every person on the plan has their own visible price. Brothers and sisters can **share the
cost** of covering their parents, so a habit that already exists in many families (everyone puts money in for the elders) becomes
transparent, automatic and fair. An insurer carries the risk; TGN designs the product, runs the plan, and buys and delivers the benefit.

## 2. The opportunity

- **The market is huge and crowded.** About 24 million South Africans have some funeral cover, and about 10.5 million hold more than one
  policy ([Capitec, via BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/banking/866861/capitec-sounds-the-alarm-over-r50-4-trillion-gap-in-south-africa/)).
  *Verified as reported; the figures come from insurers and vary by source.* People are not short of policies. They are short of
  **clarity, fairness and cover that actually fits**.
- **Funerals cost a lot, and the cost varies wildly.** Estimates run from roughly R15 000 to R50 000 on average, with urban funerals
  reported at R85 000 to R200 000 against R33 000 to R65 000 in rural areas
  ([Saturday Star](https://motoring.co.za/saturday-star/news/why-urban-funerals-are-costlier-than-rural-ones-a-closer-look-c76c69cf-7785-4d1e-8984-e13d2d43d8bf)).
  Coffins range from about R800 to over R50 000, averaging around R8 000
  ([ClearScore](https://www.clearscore.com/za/learn/insurance/how-much-does-a-funeral-cost-when-you-pay-out-of-pocket)).
  *To confirm with real supplier quotes in our first region.* That spread is where grieving families are over-charged.
- **Existing habit, no good tool.** Siblings already pool money for elders. Today it runs on trust, WhatsApp and cash.

## 3. What a customer gets (built today)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Build your own cover** | Choose a grocery hamper (the full list is visible) and add a sheep and/or a cow. Price updates live. |
| **A price for every person** | Everyone's price = base price × their relationship % × their age %. Shown, never hidden. |
| **Share the cost** | One person owns the plan and invites brothers, sisters or anyone else by phone number. Each pays a percentage; the owner pays the rest. Everyone sees who has paid. |
| **Cover only my parents** | The owner can pay without being covered themselves. |
| **Cash or bundle** | On a claim, the family can take the bundle or the cash alternative, and change their mind until it is settled. |
| **Delivery** | Staff agree a date with the family; the app shows it. |
| **After a death** | The person's cover and premium end. If it is the main member, cover for everyone else continues for 60 days while someone takes over. |
| **Plain-language rules** | Waiting periods, cooling-off, missed payments and price reviews are explained in plain words and generated from the live rules. |
| **Messages** | The customer is told, in plain words, when anything changes on their cover or claim. |

Staff run it from a web dashboard: applications, payments, claims with documents, delivery planning, collections, a profit model,
herd capacity, and an audit log. The public website has a live price builder and a waiting list.

## 4. How we win

1. **Transparency as the product.** See every item and every person's price before you apply. Our competitors sell an amount; we sell something you can count.
2. **The family plan.** Nobody else makes "cover your parents together" simple, visible and fair. It also spreads the monthly cost, which helps affordability.
3. **Speed and dignity at the worst moment.** A person calls the family, a date is agreed, and the bundle arrives. No cash to stretch, no claim forms to chase.
4. **No profit from lapses.** We will not build the business on people losing cover after missing payments.

## 5. Operating model

- **Risk sits with a licensed insurer.** TGN is the product designer, administrator and supplier. We do not carry funeral-cover risk ourselves. *Insurer not yet signed.*
- **Capital-light by design.** Groceries and livestock are bought **when a claim is made**, not stocked in advance, and livestock comes from
  contracted farmers at agreed prices. There is no inventory and no farm to buy until volumes justify it. We hold no customers' money beyond premiums.
- **Start in one region.** **North West**, supplied from Johannesburg. We know people there, which matters most for the first hundred families,
  and Johannesburg is a cheap place to buy in bulk. Many North West families have children working in Johannesburg who send money home: that
  is exactly who the family plan is for. Deaths happen at 2am, so delivery has to be proven in a place we can actually reach before we expand.
- **Later:** a partner network of undertakers at fixed, published prices (the same "no top-up" promise); tombstone and unveiling add-ons;
  funeral wishes recorded in the app; WhatsApp as a way in.

## 6. Unit economics and the guardrails that protect margin

**Where the money comes from (in order of importance):**
1. **Buying well.** The in-kind benefit is worth more to the family at shop prices than it costs us to supply. This is the core margin.
2. **What the insurer pays us** for administering and distributing the plan (commission or fee). *To negotiate.*
3. **Volume.** Fixed costs (staff, software, delivery vehicle) are spread across more plans.

**Guardrails already built into the rules** (so the business cannot lose money by accident):

| Guardrail | Why |
|---|---|
| Cash alternative is worked out **from our cost** of the bundle (a setting, default 100%) | Taking cash can never cost us more than delivering would |
| **One claim per person** (unless the first was declined) | Stops paying twice |
| **Cover and premium end** when a death claim is decided | We never charge for someone who has died; premium leakage and double payouts stop |
| A **minimum monthly share per contributor** (default R50, editable) | Every payer costs a collection fee; tiny shares would eat the margin |
| **Herd capacity** cap on livestock promises | We never promise animals the supply can't deliver |
| **Waiting periods**, a **required death certificate**, and a **review before any payout** | Standard protection against early and fraudulent claims |
| Prices **reviewed yearly with a published cap** | Inflation is handled in the open, not by surprises |
| Everything staff do is **audited** | Control and a paper trail |

**The numbers that decide whether this works (all currently placeholders):**
- Real **per-collection cost** from the payment provider. At an assumed R5, a R50 premium loses about 10% and a R145 premium about 3.4%. A family
  plan paid by three people triples the number of collections, which is why the minimum share exists.
- Real **cost to supply** each hamper, sheep and cow in the North West, from quotes.
- **Age pricing.** In the demo data a 66-year-old parent costs R81.62 a month against R29.15 for the base. Whether older parents are affordable depends
  on the insurer's real rates.
- **Claim rate** by age band, which comes from the insurer.
- **Cash alternative.** The app shows a bundle as worth, say, R8 200 at shop prices while the cash alternative is our cost (about R5 300).
  That gap is the margin. **If the rules require the cash alternative to equal the stated value, that margin disappears** (see section 7).

A profit model for staff is in the admin dashboard; it should be re-run with real numbers before any decision about money.

## 7. Regulation: what we believe, and what a lawyer must confirm

Funeral cover is insurance. The following is **our reading of public sources, not legal advice**:

- **To confirm:** funeral policies sit in a regulated class with a cap of **R100 000 per life insured**, and the regulators have said a policy in the
  *risk* class cannot provide a funeral service in kind, only money ([Joint Communication 4 of 2020](https://www.lexisnexis.co.za/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/767880/Joint-Communication-4-of-2020-Requirements-Applicable-to-Funeral-Insurance-Policies-8-May-2020-1.pdf)).
  I could not load the full document; this is from search summaries.
- **To confirm:** a funeral policy may not **require** that a cash benefit be paid straight to a service provider (the claimant may choose to direct it),
  and the insurer may not charge an admin fee when providing a non-monetary benefit (Policyholder Protection Rules, rules 2A.10.1 to 2A.10.3, seen as excerpts on
  [Acts Online](https://www.acts.co.za/long-term-/n1407_2a_4_structure_of_policy)).
- **Verified (secondary source):** waiting periods are limited (the shorter of a quarter of the term or six months for natural causes), and
  regulators are concerned about **unlicensed parlours that self-underwrite** ([Moonstone, on Joint Communication 7 of 2024](https://www.moonstone.co.za/fsca-and-pa-to-revamp-funeral-insurance-regulations-call-for-stakeholder-input/)).
  A review of how funeral insurance is distributed was opened in 2024; its outcome is **unknown to us**.

**Questions for the lawyer (in order of importance):**
1. Can the benefit be **in kind** at all, under which class, and what must the cash alternative equal?
2. What authorisation do we need to **sell and administer**: our own FSP licence, or acting under an insurer or FSP? Is a **microinsurance** route better?
3. Is a **premium payer who is not the policyholder** (the family plan) permitted, and what consent is needed from the **people being covered**?
4. What must the website, the app and any video say and not say before we open?

## 8. Risks

| Risk | What we do |
|---|---|
| In-kind benefits turn out not to be allowed, or cash must equal value | Legal opinion **before** any more product spend; the cash option is already built so the model can flex |
| No insurer signs | Waiting list proves demand; approach several insurers and managing agents with real numbers |
| Delivery fails at 2am | One region; contracted suppliers; a duty phone; prove it before expanding |
| Payment fees eat the margin | Real quote, minimum share, fewer larger payers; negotiate volume pricing |
| Suppliers raise prices | Yearly price review with a published cap; supplier agreements; cost tracked per claim |
| Old parents are unaffordable | Insurer's real age rates; family sharing; smaller bundles for older ages |
| A mistake with someone's data | Encrypted ID numbers, audit log, delete-my-account, privacy by default |
| Reputation if we get a funeral wrong | Plain communication, a person calls the family, nothing is automated that shouldn't be |

## 10. What is built and what is next

**Built and tested** (backend, customer app, staff dashboard, public website; 83 automated backend tests; the database migrates on PostgreSQL):
accounts and phone verification; the cover builder and live price; family plans; cover-only-my-parents; payments and statements;
claims with documents, cash or bundle choice and delivery planning; after-a-death rules; collections; messages; the waiting list; Flyway migrations; the profit model.

**Not built yet:** a real payment provider and debit orders (staff record payments by hand today); an SMS provider (codes only log in development);
staff roles beyond one admin; reports and exports; isiZulu and Sesotho; an undertaker partner network; tombstone and funeral-wishes features; production hosting and phone-store builds.

## 11. Parked, on purpose

- A pooled **family fund** (for medication and other elder care). It would mean holding members' money, which is regulated differently. It could come
  later once the core product has revenue and a payment partner.
- **Coffin tiers** priced per person (Essential, Dignity, Heritage) with an "or equivalent" promise. Worth doing after the legal answer in section 7.
- **WhatsApp-first** sign-up and reminders.
